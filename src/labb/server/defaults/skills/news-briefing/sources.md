# Judging news sources

## Usually strong
- **Wire services and newsrooms that do their own reporting**: named reporters, datelines, corrections policies, quotes from people they spoke to.
- **Primary sources**: official statements, court documents, regulator filings, government statistics, scientific papers, company investor releases. Link them next to the coverage.
- **Specialist press** for its own field (trade publications, science journals' news sections), when the story is in that field.
- **Local outlets** for local events: they're often first and closest.

## Treat with care
- **Aggregators and rewrites**: follow them back to the original and cite that.
- **Opinion, analysis and editorials**: fine for "why it matters", but label them as opinion and never as fact.
- **Press releases**: the company's own claims. Say "the company says".
- **Social media posts**: only as "X posted ...", and only from verified accounts of the people involved.
- **Live blogs**: early details there often change; check the latest update.

## Leave out
- Pages with no author, no date, or no sources for their claims.
- Content farms, clickbait headlines that the article doesn't back up, sites built around ads.
- Anything that only one anonymous source supports, unless you say exactly that.

## Signs of a good article
Named author, publish and update dates, links or references to its sources, quotes attributed by name, a clear line between news and opinion, and corrections when it got something wrong.
