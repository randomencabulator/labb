# News briefing

Give the reader what happened, from sources they can trust, with dates.

## Steps

1. **Pin down the ask.** Topic, region, and time window. With no window, use the last 7 days; for a fast story, the last 24 hours.
2. **Search wide.** Run 2-4 `web_search` queries with different wording (the topic, the key names, "latest", the month and year). Use `read` on the first search to get pages at once.
3. **Pick sources by quality**, not by rank. Read `sources.md` for what counts as good. Prefer the original reporting over pages that rewrite it, and the primary source (the court filing, the company's statement, the official data) when there is one.
4. **Check the dates.** Note each article's publish date. Leave out anything older than the window unless it's needed as background, and say it's background.
5. **Cross-check.** A claim only one outlet reports gets "reported by X, not confirmed elsewhere". Where sources disagree, say so and give both.
6. **Write the briefing** (format below). Facts first, no padding, no opinions of your own.

## Format

```
## <Topic>: briefing for <date>

**In short:** 2-3 sentences: the most important thing, and where it stands now.

### What happened
- <fact>, <when> ([Source](url), <date>)
- ...

### Why it matters
1-3 bullets, attributed when it's someone's analysis.

### Still unclear
What isn't known yet or is disputed. Leave the section out if nothing is.

### Sources
- [Title](url), outlet, date
```

Keep it under about 300 words unless asked for more. When nothing new happened in the window, say so plainly and give the latest real development with its date.
